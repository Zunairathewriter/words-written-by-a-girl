Pakistan Independence Day? Not for women

Pakistani men should be ashamed of themselves

14 August is Pakistan's Independence Day — the day Pakistan gained independence from British rule in 1947.

That's the day Pakistan became an independent country, and everyone celebrates it. Flags go up, songs play, people wear green. But honestly, this day doesn't mean much to me.

For men, yes, this country is independent. They can live here freely, speak their minds, walk outside at any hour, make choices about their own lives without asking permission. But what about women?

In my opinion, this country is still not truly independent — at least not for women. Nothing has really changed for us. If anything, it feels like it's gotten worse. If this country were still under British rule right now, I honestly think it would be better. At least women would be living on their own terms. At least they'd wear whatever they wanted without being stared at or judged. At least they'd be fighting for their rights instead of being told to stay quiet. At least people would be more open-minded. At least men would be held accountable for their mistakes instead of being excused for them. At least women would push back when a man looked at them the wrong way, instead of being taught that silence is safer.

But what actually happens when a man looks at a woman the wrong way? She covers herself up more. She shrinks. She apologises for existing in a body. Women grow up not even knowing their own rights — not knowing what they're legally or humanly entitled to, because no one ever tells them, and no one ever asks them what they want. They cook, they clean, they raise children, they give birth again and again — even when they're exhausted, even when no one notices or cares. They keep the household running even on the days their own bodies are begging for rest, even during their periods, still cooking, still cleaning, still serving everyone else first. And somehow they're made to believe that whatever is said to them, however they're spoken to, is something they brought on themselves — that they must have done something wrong to deserve it.

There are homes in this country where the way a husband treats his wife would horrify anyone looking in from outside, and yet inside those same walls, it's brushed off as just how marriage is. I won't go into detail — some things are better left unsaid than repeated — but I think most people reading this already know exactly what I mean.

And if a man touches a woman inappropriately in public, she hides it. She stays silent. She doesn't report it, doesn't fight back — because if she does, people will say she must have done something, she must have been dressed provocatively, she must have invited it — even when she was fully covered from head to toe.

Here, women are so often treated as if their comfort, safety, and dignity are optional. It's considered almost normal for men to look at women the wrong way, to touch them the wrong way, and for the woman to be the one who ends up ashamed. Women can't be independent because they're rarely given the room to be. This is a country where a daughter can be earning her own money, helping support her entire family, and her father will still hide it — too afraid of what the neighbors might think. But if a son brings home even a small amount, the whole family will announce it proudly to everyone they know.

I'm only writing half of what there is to say. There's so much more — but maybe some of it is better left for another day.

And if you're reading this and thinking I'm writing it because I'm going through it myself — no, I'm not. But this is simply how this country can be for so many women, even if it's not talked about openly. That said, I want to be clear: there are a lot of good people here too. Not all men are the same, and not all women are the same. This isn't about blaming an entire gender — it's about naming a pattern that's been allowed to continue for far too long.

But I want to end on this — I do think it's slowly getting better. More women are speaking up. More people are listening. And that gives me hope.

But it's still not good enough that men think that they can tell women what to wear, and the shocking part is that they listen and don't remind men of their place. And let me tell one more thing that used to happen in this country before — when baby girls were born, many baby girls were killed by their own fathers. It stopped a long time ago. I don't know if this thing still happens.

So many Pakistani men should be ashamed of themselves; not all of them, but yeah, mostly all of them.
