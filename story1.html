Every story begins with a feeling. But mine begins with my thoughts.

The World Behind Closed Eyes

There's a whole universe behind closed eyes, my dear.

The wind came in through the open window and moved across her face like a gentle hand. She sat at her desk, a blank page in front of her, and tried — for the hundredth time that night — to write about freedom.

Not the grand kind. The quiet kind. The kind you find inside a single room.

She had been trying for hours. The words never came the right way. She leaned back in her chair and let everything go — her arms, her legs, the weight in her neck — until she was just a body sitting in the dark, staring at the ceiling, asking herself the only question that seemed to matter tonight.

How does it feel to be free when you never leave your room?

The cold wind kept coming. It touched her pink cheeks, moved through her hair, and slowly unknotted something tight in her chest. She closed her light brown eyes. Opened them. Closed them again — thinking, half-believing, that ideas would come if she just looked hard enough at the darkness behind her eyelids. But tiredness is heavier than hope, and slowly, like stones being placed one by one, sleep pressed down on her.

She pulled her knees to her chest. Turned to her left side. And fell asleep in her chair — still wearing her thoughts, still holding the question — as the night grew darker and the wind grew cold.

When she closed her eyes in this world, she opened them in another.

She was sitting against the trunk of a tree. Grass was soft and cool beneath her. The sky above was a clear, wide light-blue — the kind of blue that has never held a single cloud. Birds were singing somewhere close, and then closer still, until one landed on her shoulder. She held her breath. Slowly, she lifted her finger.

The bird stepped onto it without fear.

It turned its small head to the left, then to the right, studying her with gentle eyes. Then it opened its wings and was gone — not frightened, just free. She smiled watching it go, the kind of smile that starts small and keeps growing until your whole face is part of it.

She stood and walked, and the world opened ahead of her. A lake appeared, still and clear as a mirror. She stepped toward it slowly, and when she looked down, she almost laughed.

She did not recognize herself — and yet she knew exactly who she was.

Her hair hung loose and shining, free in a way it never was at home. She wore a simple dress and a pair of sand-coloured shoes, and on her head, a crown made of flowers. She looked at her reflection for a long moment. Then she smiled at herself — not to check how she looked, but simply because she was glad to be there.

A deer was watching her from the grass nearby.

She expected it to run. It didn't. Instead, it stepped toward her, lowered its head, and waited. She reached out and rested her hand between its ears. The deer closed its eyes. A second deer came from the trees, gentle and unhurried, and pressed its forehead softly to hers.

Tears came — not from sadness, but from something she didn't have a name for. The feeling of being trusted by something that did not have to trust you. The feeling of being seen, quietly, with no judgment at all.

She stood there with her eyes wet and her heart full, watching them walk back into the tall grass, until they were just shapes, and then just memory.

The sun began to set. The sky turned pink and purple at the edges — colours so soft they looked like something painted with watercolour on wet paper. She found a large flat stone beside the lake, wide enough to sit on comfortably, and she sat.

She had always planned to do this. Watch a sunset without rushing. Without someone asking something of her. Without a phone in her hand. She had always told herself: one day, when life settles.

Today was that day. Except she was asleep in a chair.

She didn't look away. The sky moved slowly through every colour it knew. She didn't blink. She did not want to miss even a second of it — because no one here would block her view, and no one here would reach for their phone.

She would carry this in her heart. That would be enough.

Then she heard it.

A low, soft sound from behind her. Not quite a meow — deeper than that, but still gentle. She turned.

A black cat sat at the edge of the treeline. Heavy fur. Still as stone. Its eyes were yellow, the colour of old gold, and they looked at her not with curiosity but with something more careful than that. A question. Who are you, and why are you here?

The cat did not move for a long moment. Then it blinked — slow, deliberate, unbothered — turned, and walked back into the forest. It moved the way things move when they have never been afraid of anything in their lives. She watched until the trees swallowed it completely, then let out a breath she hadn't known she was holding.

She was still smiling when the stars came out.

The night arrived without warning, and the wind near the lake turned sharp and cold. She was shivering before she noticed — then she turned and saw a coat folded neatly on the stone behind her. Heavy wool. Warm. The colour of deep winter. She looked around. No one.

She put it on. It matched her dress perfectly.

She laughed a little at that — at the simple, impossible generosity of a dream that gives you exactly what you need before you know you need it. Here, things didn't have to make sense to be real. Here, the laws were different. Kind, even. The kind of laws written not to punish, but to protect. No one here was made into a villain. No one needed to be saved.

She sat against a tree and pulled the coat tighter. Above her, the moon was so bright it cast thin shadows through the branches. The stars stretched across the whole sky like something someone had scattered by hand. She had no words for them. She didn't try to find any. She just looked.

For the first time in a long time, she smiled not because she wanted someone to see it — but because she couldn't help it. Her heart was quiet. Her mind was quiet. Even her body felt different, as if it had remembered how to rest.

I didn't know I could feel like this, she thought. I didn't know I was allowed to.

She closed her eyes. The moonlight fell across her face, and she did not turn away from it. Usually, the light made it hard to sleep. But this light was different. It felt like warmth. Like a hand resting gently on your forehead.

She let it hold her there, in the forest, under the stars — and fell into a deeper, quieter sleep inside her sleep.

A different light woke her. Thin and gold, coming through her bedroom window. Early morning. The birds were singing again — real birds this time, on real branches — and somewhere outside a cat was calling in that low, certain way cats have.

She was in her chair. Her neck was a little stiff. Her page was still blank.

She sat up slowly and looked at the morning, and she thought about the lake and the deer and the coat and the cat with the golden eyes. She thought about how it felt to sit under stars with no one asking anything of her. She knew, in the quiet logic of dreams, that she might not find that place again. Dreams like that — the ones that feel like gifts — they don't always come back when you look for them.

But she was glad. Truly, fully glad — not the kind of glad you perform for other people, but the kind that lives in your chest and asks nothing in return.

She picked up her pen.

And she began to write.
